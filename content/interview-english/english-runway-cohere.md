# Interview English: how Runway and Cohere leaders actually speak

Date: 2026-10-23. One consolidated study sheet built from about 300,000 words of real speech: six people measured closely, 41 more Cohere videos read for vocabulary. Everything here comes from transcripts, not from textbooks. Quotes are short phrases for practice; the source research and full transcripts live in `~/ClaudeCode Projects/Runaway/` (see section 12).

The same file lives in two repos: `InterviewPreparationsBot/research/speaking/english-runway-cohere.md` and `Language-Learning-Bot/content/interview-english/english-runway-cohere.md`. The language repo also holds `runway-cohere-vocab-seed.json`, about 100 cards in the `{term, context, source}` shape the planned Vocab SRS uses.

## TL;DR

- The bar is C1 discourse, not perfect grammar. Every non-native founder makes article, plural and tense errors about once a minute, and no host ever reacts.
- Fillers are normal. The two native Cohere founders fill more than the Runway founders: "like" up to 26 per 1,000 words, "um/uh" up to 33.
- What separates levels is structure and nouns: a frame at the start of each answer, a signpost for lists, a contrast, a hedge, a landing line, and enough domain words that you never stop to search.
- Grammar is small: four tenses, three ways to talk about the future, three conditional shapes, about fifteen constructions. Skip the perfect tenses; nobody uses them.
- Each leader has one signature move worth copying: Alejandro's extended metaphor, Anastasis's "versus" and research hedges, Cristóbal's rhetorical question chains, Gomez's concede-then-split, Frosst's agree-then-correct.

What the table says:
1. Fillers are a native habit. A filler every 30 to 50 words is normal at CEO level.
2. "Would" at about 2 per 1,000 is the native floor for hypotheticals. Alejandro at zero is the outlier, and it makes every hypothesis sound like a fact.
3. "Versus" is Anastasis's personal habit, not an English norm. Natives contrast with "but" and "instead".
4. Past simple carries stories, present continuous carries current work. Present perfect is rare everywhere.

## 3. Discourse: what makes C1 and C2

Three layers decide how you sound: words (do you have the noun), grammar (is each sentence right) and discourse (how the whole answer is organised). The founders are B1 to B2 on grammar and C1 on discourse. Discourse is the layer to train.

The listener test: at B2 the listener assembles your answer, at C1 they follow it, at C2 they repeat it later.

**The five C1 moves.** Every long answer should contain all five.
1. **Frame**: say what kind of answer is coming. "The way that I see it is..."
2. **Signpost**: number the parts. "There are three reasons. One is..."
3. **Contrast**: put two things side by side. "...versus if you train from scratch."
4. **Hedge**: claim with the right confidence. "We have no indication that this is saturating."
5. **Land**: end on a short line. "So it's a matter of time before..."

**The C2 moves.** These make people quote you.
- **Name your own idea**: "I call it the lucid dream test." (Anastasis)
- **Extend one metaphor**: carving a stone into a sculpture, for two minutes. (Alejandro)
- **Argue by analogy or trend**: "That's just GPT-2." (Anastasis) The printing press, photography as "mirrors with memories". (Cristóbal)
- **Reframe the question**: "It's less about X, it's more about Y." (Cristóbal)
- **Voice the objector**, then answer: "People said, whoa, too many stories." (Cristóbal)
- **Ask three questions in a row**: "Is that a game? Is that a film? Is that a phone call?" (Cristóbal)
- **Switch register** inside one turn: "we optimized the hell out of it" next to "step-distilled autoregressive model". (Anastasis)
- **Land on a fragment or imperative**: "Crazy. Unfathomable." (Frosst) "Figure it out." (Cristóbal)

## 4. Grammar and constructions

### 4a. What each tense is for

| Tense | Job | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present simple, generic "you" | Principles and definitions | "You go there for one thing and one thing only." |
| Present continuous | Current work and trends | "We're exploring...", "we're seeing users stitching clips together" |
| Present perfect | Lessons and track record | "What we have learned is...", "we've been making foundational models since then" |
| Past simple | Stories | "We made a big bet." "It became clear that..." |
| Historic present | Drama inside a story | "At that point, February 2024, Sora comes out." |
| "used to" | The old way | "People used to write incredibly complex prompts." |

### 4b. The future, three ways

- **going to / gonna** for predictions and trends: "There's gonna be more movies." The default for everyone except Cristóbal.
- **will** for rules, commitments and certainty: "We will always have a human in the loop." "Nothing will change." Cristóbal uses it to state laws.
- **Present continuous** for plans already running: "We're doing a summit." "We're hiring across the board."

### 4c. Conditionals and hypotheticals

- Real: if + present, then gonna. "If scaling laws apply on video just like on language, then they're gonna simulate physics."
- Hypothetical: if + past, would. "If we trained a video model at scale, we would get a great model."
- Soft claim, no "if" needed: "I would say...", "I would expect a year from now..."
- Past counterfactual, spoken style: "If it wasn't us, someone else would have done it." (Gomez)
- Formal inversion, rare: "Had it existed, maybe I would have gone there." (Frosst)

### 4d. Constructions they reuse

| Construction | Example |
|---|---|
| preposition + -ing | "by testing, by constantly exploring", "we invested in building control" |
| need to / have to | "We need to be able to visualize them first." |
| can + see / tell / get | "You can immediately tell with your eyes." |
| there is + noun | "There is no reason why it shouldn't work." |
| passive for process | "It was trained on self-driving datasets." |
| when + clause, main clause | "When the camera was invented, we never anticipated cinema." |
| the moment you X, Y | "The moment you open it, it's an open-ended world." |
| it's less about X, more about Y | "It's less about making things 10 percent better." |
| the more X, the more Y | "The more I build it, the more I'm like, this is the right approach." |
| a lot more like X than Y | "Building LLMs is a lot more like building a rocket than other software." |
| negative raising | "I don't think they pose a threat like that." (never "I think they don't") |
| nor + inversion | "Nor do I care." |
| cleft | "What we're seeing is...", "The thing about X is that..." |
| rather than + -ing | "...rather than going too far on the purely design phase." |
| in order to | "In order to predict video well, you need to simulate the world." |
| as X as we can | "as fast as we can", "as many things as you can" |
| way + comparative | "way more people", "way faster than before" |
| be like (quotative) | "And I was like, oh my god, he's here." |

### 4e. Skip these, nobody uses them

Past perfect, reported speech with backshift, "not only did we", future perfect, future continuous, "must". In about 300,000 words they appear zero times or almost never.

## 5. Phrase bank by function

Initials: AM Alejandro Matamala, AG Anastasis Germanidis, CV Cristóbal Valenzuela, GZ Aidan Gomez, NF Nick Frosst, EC Elliott Choi.

**Opening an answer**
- "Yeah, I think..." (NF, GZ, nearly every answer)
- "Yeah. Well, ..." (GZ)
- "Yeah. A hundred percent. And we're trying to go beyond that." (AM)
- "Correct. Correct." / "Of course." before expanding (CV)
- "Not quite." as a full first sentence (AG)
- "I mean, our objective is..." (NF)

**Setting the stage and defining terms**
- "Let's spend some time talking about what we mean by X first." (NF)
- "So just to set the stage a little bit..." (NF)
- "Let me first talk a little bit about..." (NF)
- "If you think about it from a base level..." (CV)
- "The first thing to say is that..." (GZ)
- "The way that I see it is like..." (AM)
- "The way we approach it at our company is..." (GZ)

**Signposting**
- "There are three different reasons for that. One is... The second reason is... And the third..." (AM)
- "A couple of things had to happen. The first one..." (CV)
- "The other thing is that..." (GZ)
- "There's a few parts to being New York." (AG)

**Contrasting**
- "The big difference is..." (NF)
- "The way that we differ is that we're not trying to build the biggest thing." (NF)
- "Instead, we're making..." (NF)
- "There is one way of seeing those models, which is X, and there is the other way, which is Y." (AG)
- "X versus Y", "compared to", "whereas" (AG)
- "It's less about X. It's more about Y." (CV)
- "I don't think it's a matter of X. I think it's a function of Y." (AM)

**Hedging**
- Design hedge: "I think...", "kind of", "a little bit", "maybe" (AM)
- Research hedge: "In my mind...", "My sense is...", "I would say...", "I would expect..." (AG)
- Research certainty: "We have no indication that this is saturating." "There is no reason why it shouldn't work." "I'll be very surprised if, two years from now, we don't..." "I feel very strongly that..." (AG)
- Native adverb hedge: probably, pretty, largely, certainly, essentially (NF, GZ)
- "I don't know whether..." (GZ)
- "Chances are..." (NF)
- "If we are just purely speculating, I think..." (AM)

**Conceding, then disagreeing**
- "Models have certainly gotten better. And the real step change has been..." (NF)
- "That's a good term. A lot of the thought around it feels religious to me." (NF)
- "I would say it's more like..." (NF)
- "No, that's a little extreme." (NF)
- "It's important that we think about that, but I really don't think..." (GZ)
- "They've done a lot of good work, but I'm interested in something different." (GZ)
- "I don't want to presume their intentions, but certainly you could imagine a scenario where..." (GZ)
- "That being said, ..." (NF)

**Declining a question gracefully**
- "I am not an expert in policy. What I am an expert in is what this technology can do and how it's built." (NF)
- "I don't have the right answer to say, but I have no doubt that..." (AM)
- "We'll figure it out." (CV)

**Predicting**
- "It's just a matter of time before..." (AG)
- "It was more a matter of when, not if." (AG)
- "I see a scenario where..." (AM)
- "My prediction is..." / "My position is..." (CV)
- "I think this technology is going to get boring." (NF)

**Telling a dated story**
- "Back in 2016, 2017..." (AG)
- "At that point, February 2024, X comes out." (AG)
- "Very quickly, we realized..." / "It then became clear that..." (AG)
- "That was a big turning point." (AG)
- "Back then...", "At the time...", "Eventually..." (CV)
- "Something fun that I haven't really talked about is..." (AG)

**Giving an analogy or example**
- "One way to think about it is..." (AG)
- "It's kind of like asking what is the best use case for a word processor." (NF)
- "In the same way that there's not that many companies building rockets." (NF)
- "I'll go to an extreme example." / "Put yourself in the beginning of the 1900s." (CV)
- "To me, it's a form of what sampling is in hip hop." (AM)

**Landing**
- "So I think..." (NF)
- "That's kind of the point." (CV)
- "Who cares how it was made? Did it work as a story or not?" (CV)
- "We're building ROI, not AGI." (NF)
- "Just render. Why generate the code that generates the pixels?" (AG)
- "That's a success. And I think we'll get paid along the way." (GZ)
- Fragments: "Crazy. Unfathomable." (NF) "Shocking." (GZ)

**Reacting to the interviewer**
- "Yeah, exactly." / "For sure." / "A hundred percent."
- "It's a good question too." / "Good questions."
- "Thank you for having me."

## 9. Errors that cost nothing

All of these came from founders on recorded interviews. No host reacted once.

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Articles | "an hypothesis", "a LLM", "a indication", "it was definitely adjustment period" |
| Plurals | "should designer code or not", "different type of skills", "most of the times" |
| Agreement | "there is companies", "who come from Chile", "a lot of things needs to happen", "people in California is like" |
| Tense | "I just become a father", "we spin up this research group", "he didn't told his colleagues" |
| Prepositions | "thinking in moving images", "we are seeking for", "spend money in it" |
| Word shape | "interiorize", "sunken cost", "carefuler", "less steps", "a lot more harder" |
| Natives too | "there's people", "you need less people", "privacy and security is essential", "what capsule networks was trying to do" |

Rule: do not stop to fix these mid-sentence. A restart costs more than the error.

## 10. The checklist

Four bars. Work them in order.

**Design round (Alejandro level)**
1. Talk 45 minutes without searching for words. Use "kind of", "a little bit", "I think", "right?" as buffers while the next clause loads.
2. Open every answer with a frame from section 5.
3. Say "going to" or "gonna" for predictions. Keep "will" for rules and promises.
4. Own the domain words in 7a and 7h.
5. Have one metaphor you can extend for two minutes. His is carving a stone. Yours must be your own.
6. Use "rather than", "in order to", "by + -ing", "as X as we can" once per long answer.
7. Recognize the interviewer phrases in section 8 on the fly.
8. Stop fixing articles, plurals and tense mid-sentence.

**Research round (Anastasis level)**
9. Contrast with "versus", "compared to", "whereas".
10. Use research nouns as verbs: condition a model on, distill it, post-train it, roll out a policy.
11. Argue by analogy and trend: "that's just GPT-2", "no indication that this is saturating".
12. Hedge like a researcher: "in my mind", "my sense is", "I would expect", "it's a matter of time before".
13. Name your own thought experiment and your own one-liner.
14. Tell a dated story with a turning point.

**CEO round (Cristóbal level)**
15. Ask three rhetorical questions in a row, then answer them.
16. Voice the objector in direct speech, then knock it down.
17. Keep three historical analogies ready; use one per long answer.
18. Say "it's less about X, it's more about Y" once per answer.
19. Land on an imperative.
20. "Will" for rules you believe, "going to" for scenes you describe.

**Native round (Gomez and Frosst level)**
21. Treat fillers as beats, not faults.
22. Open with "Yeah" and a verdict, then build.
23. Hedge with adverbs: probably, pretty, largely, certainly.
24. Negative raising: "I don't think X is Y".
25. Land with a fragment after a long chain.
26. Disagree by agreeing first, correcting the category and naming no person.
27. Decline by naming your expertise.
28. Memorise 20 idioms from section 6. Everything else is structure.
29. Every hypothetical gets a "would".
